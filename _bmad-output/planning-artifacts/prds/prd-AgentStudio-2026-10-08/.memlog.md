- (decision) D1 Centre of gravity = 'Reasoning is broad; authority is bounded.' Internal thesis = one capable Agent system, many business specifications. North-star = a small business can 'hire' and configure an AI worker. V1 = prove short onboarding produces an Agent that behaves like a worker for that business. Three layers kept distinct, not collapsed.
- (decision) D2 'General-purpose' is NOT the customer-facing differentiator. Correct architecture, poor market message. Sell the outcome; keep general-purpose as internal architecture claim.
- (decision) D3 Templates: NO workflow template library. YES pre-filled Business Specifications as onboarding accelerators (e.g. 'dental clinic' seeds terminology, common requests, questions to ask, policies to consider). Opposed to template-DEFINED workflows as the source of agent behaviour, not to templates as spec starting points. Avoid blank-canvas onboarding without becoming a template marketplace.
- (decision) D4 Builder user = role archetype 'Business operator / owner-operator', NOT a fictional named persona (no evidence to invent one). Defining characteristic is possession of the business's actual operational knowledge incl. undocumented rules - not company size. Buyer=builder often true in practice but must NOT become an architectural requirement.
- (decision) D5 Falsifiable posture carried forward strongly. PRD must not assume architecture or usefulness claims are proven. V1 is explicitly a validation/build phase. Central claim to prove: can a Business Specification produced by short onboarding drive RELIABLE agent behaviour across materially different businesses. The four exemplars demonstrate architectural diversity, they are NOT evidence the system works - preserve that distinction. User preference: smaller PRD with falsifiable acceptance criteria over a confident-looking PRD that assumes the hard part is solved.
- (decision) D6 ELICITATION WINS over minimality - called the most important product decision. 'Minimal Builder' redefined: minimal burden and UI complexity FOR THE USER, never minimal reasoning BY the Builder. Builder must actively interview the operator when important business behaviour is missing or ambiguous. Targets: tacit rules, exceptions, thresholds, prohibitions, authority limits, 'we normally do X except when', printed-vs-practised policy gaps, decision prerequisites, always-escalate cases. This is a core V1 capability, NOT a later enhancement.
- (decision) D7 Builder must NOT become a questionnaire. Elicitation stays conversational and adaptive, not a 100-question form. Opens with 'Tell me about your business and what you want the Agent to handle' and decides what needs clarification from what the operator says and supplies. The platform's Agent nature must be visible on the Builder side too.
- (decision) D8 V1 onboarding model of record: optional starting point -> conversational elicitation -> generated Business Specification + Knowledge Artifacts -> validation/self-test. Explicitly NOT 'pick template -> fill fields -> deploy'.
- (override) D9 Model developments (Haiku 5.5 economics of cheap high-volume calls; OpenAI Decisions API; TypeSafe/LangChain Jev typed decision models) are IMPLEMENTATION OPPORTUNITIES ONLY -> addendum, never PRD commitments. Multi-model routing = implementation/evaluation question, not a V1 product requirement. No architectural dependency on any of them. Provider agnosticism remains a hard boundary. Conceptual takeaway kept: 'Not every intelligent decision needs the full Agent loop.'
- (decision) D10 Novelty claims FORBIDDEN: model-in-charge agents, harnesses, tool calling, declarative agent configuration, business rules as data - all established prior art. The narrow falsifiable thesis instead: 'A non-engineer can describe how their business actually operates, including tacit rules and boundaries, and the platform turns that into a business-specific Agent that behaves correctly across real conversations.' Agentforce Script / MS Foundry / Sierra / Oracle OAS to be named as prior art in the PRD's own competitive section.
- (decision) D11 The question the PRD must answer: 'Can we make the transition from "this is my business" to "this Agent behaves like someone who works here" short, reliable, and repeatable across different businesses?' PRD must not drift into framework selection, policy-engine selection, durability design, or novel orchestration.

- (decision) D12 V1 CHANNEL = inbound email/messaging. Secondary benefit: near-zero latency pressure removes a class of risk from V1. This resolves Spine deferral B6, which required the PRD to pick while keeping all behaviour channel-neutral.
