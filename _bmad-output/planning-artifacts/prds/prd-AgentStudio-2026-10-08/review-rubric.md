# PRD Quality Review — Agent Studio Business-Specific Agent Platform

*Reviewed: `prd.md` (586 lines, FR1–FR98, NFR1–NFR18, O1–O9, E1–E12) and `addendum.md` (A1–A3), as of the state on disk at review time. Note: both documents were being edited during this review — §7.4, FR95–FR98 and addendum §A3 appeared mid-read and are included; §12 and §13 had not yet absorbed them, which accounts for several findings below.*

## Overall verdict

This is a genuinely strong internal spec on the dimensions that are usually faked — it has a real thesis, states what would falsify it, names its prior art, refuses to invent numbers it does not have, and carries an operational limitations half that most PRDs leave empty. The deliberate omissions flagged as context (no numeric targets, role archetype instead of persona, expressive capability instead of a field schema) are all handled well rather than merely present: each is stated as a decision with the reason and a revisit condition, not left as a hole.

What is at risk is the inverse of what the PRD claims about itself. The document names §8.1 Builder elicitation as *"the product risk"* and E2 as the highest-risk epic, but §8.1 is the least buildable section in the document: FR3, FR4, FR5, FR7 and FR10 have no done-condition an implementer could test, and FR12 instruments Builder **burden** (time, artifact count) while nothing instruments Builder **yield** (did elicitation actually surface the undocumented rules). The hard thing is specified at the lowest resolution in the document while the Core — the settled, lower-risk part — is specified sharply enough to build from today.

Second-order risk is a set of real internal contradictions, not stylistic ones: the improvement loop has no authoring step (§7.2 excludes the specification editor and no FR gives the Builder an edit path), escalation in V1 lands nowhere (FR47/FR69 versus §7.2's excluded escalation inbox and O2), FR76 overclaims computability against §10.3, and FR97 flatly contradicts §13.2's concurrency paragraph. None of these is fatal; all of them will be discovered expensively during story breakdown if not closed first.

**On the specific check requested:** no requirement in FR1–FR98 specifies a business-level sequence. The closest candidates are all correctly guarded — FR60 is a platform lifecycle with business sub-states explicitly forbidden, FR50 is parking/resumption rather than ordering, FR15 and FR14's *"what must be established before each outcome or action"* are precondition sets explicitly distinguished from ordering by FR18 and FR24, and FR53's confirmation-before-authorization is a Core gate. The §6 forbidden-vocabulary list and the §5 standing self-check are doing real work here. This dimension of the PRD is clean.

## Decision-readiness — strong

A decision-maker can act on this. Decisions are stated as decisions and located where a reader looks for them: §1.2 separates the architecture claim from the north-star framing from the V1 objective and explicitly marks the middle one *"Not a V1 commercial promise"*; §7.2 gives every exclusion a reason rather than a shrug; §7.3 names eleven deferrals and states the rule that governs them (*"the requirement states the guarantee and names no mechanism"*). Trade-offs are named with what was given up — §4.2's *"No moat is claimed. ... Commercial potential is unproven, and the project is not justified by it"* is the kind of sentence most PRDs in this genre cannot produce.

The strongest decision-readiness move is §4.4, which states the loudest objection to the product's shape in the objector's own terms (*"A competitor's deterministic pitch is a genuine feature to an operator whose business depends on not contacting the wrong customer"*) and then answers it structurally rather than by assertion. Someone pushing back would find their objection acknowledged. §10.4's counter-metrics are the same discipline applied to the metrics: every headline number has a named way it could be gamed.

The open items in §11 are genuinely open — O1 (who reviews), O2 (where escalation lands), O3 (disclosure) have no answer smuggled into the next sentence, and each carries a revisit condition tied to a project event rather than a date. Open-items density (9 open items, 0 `[ASSUMPTION]`, 0 `[NOTE FOR PM]`) is low for a document at this stakes level — appropriate, since this PRD is downstream of a settled architecture rather than a green-light-to-build gate.

### Findings

- **medium** FR11's human review is asserted but never made a gate (§8.3, §8.1) — FR11 says *"A human reviews the drafted specification before it may be promoted"*, but FR22's hard gate is the validation suite only, and no requirement demands a recorded review verdict, approver identity, or that promotion be blocked without one. O1 defers *who* reviews, which is legitimate; but *whether human approval is an enforced gate producing an artifact* is a product decision, not an architecture one, and it is currently unstated. An implementer building promotion will either guess or omit it. *Fix:* state in FR11 whether review approval is a blocking precondition and whether it is recorded as attributable evidence (FR28 implies yes); leave only the reviewer's identity and competence to O1.
- **low** §11 promises an owner column it does not have (§11 lead line) — *"Each has an owner and a revisit condition rather than an invented answer"*, but the table columns are Open item / Why it is open / Revisit when. No owner appears for any of O1–O9. *Fix:* add the owner column or drop the claim.

## Substance over theater — strong

Almost nothing here is furniture, and the document actively polices the failure modes the rubric names. There is no persona theater: §2 states *"A role archetype, deliberately not a named persona — no primary operator evidence has been obtained yet, and inventing a persona would manufacture confidence the research does not support"*, and the three secondary roles are listed as roles with their open questions attached rather than dressed up as personas. There is no innovation theater: §4.1 opens by listing what is **not** claimed as novel (*"model-in-charge agents, agent harnesses, tool calling, declarative agent configuration, business rules represented as data"*) and §4.2 names six pieces of shipping prior art including the one most damaging to a broad version of the claim. A differentiation section that argues its own claim *down* to a defensible width is the opposite of theater.

The NFRs mostly escape NFR theater by an explicit device: the fourth column forces each one to declare its quantification status, and three of them (NFR6, NFR13, NFR14) say the value is deliberately unset and name what will set it. NFR15 is the honest limit case — *"Not V1 concerns, and stated as such"* — which is a legitimate answer under the §1.7-style bar rather than a dodge. The Vision is not swappable: §1.3's *"this is my business"* → *"this Agent behaves like someone who works here"*, with **short / reliable / repeatable** each mapped to a requirement class, could not drop into another PRD unchanged.

§6's forbidden-vocabulary list (*workflow, state machine, business transition graph, workflow language, execution graph, stage, node*) is the clearest evidence the document's constraints are load-bearing rather than decorative — it creates a mechanical test a reviewer can run against any future edit.

### Findings

- **low** §1.4 and §4.1 state the same narrowed claim twice (§1.4, §4.1) — near-identical content (*"a non-engineer can describe how their business actually operates…"* versus *"a specification that is declarative about the business… authored in business language by a non-engineer"*). Not harmful, but the reader cannot tell which is canonical. *Fix:* make §4.1 a pointer to §1.4 and keep the prior-art table as §4's contribution.

## Strategic coherence — adequate

The PRD has a thesis and the thesis drives prioritization, which is the main thing. §1.4 states the narrow claim, §5 states what would falsify it, and §12's closing line follows from it rather than from convenience: *"Two epics carry the V1 claim: E2, because elicitation is the hard problem, and E12, because without it this is a runtime plus an assertion that it can be configured."* That is thesis-driven sequencing, not easy-first. The MVP scope kind is a platform/validation scope and the scope logic matches it — S11 makes the generalisation programme a V1 deliverable (*"V1 is not complete until these exist"*) rather than a post-launch nicety, which is the correct shape for a document whose job is to falsify two claims.

The Success Metrics validate the thesis rather than measuring activity: §10.3's three measurements map one-to-one onto the architecture claim's falsifiers, and §10.4 exists specifically to stop the §10.2 numbers from being gamed. §10.2's *"reported separately from the share blocked by the business's own rules or capacity"* is a genuine insight about this product, not a generic metric.

Coherence breaks in one specific place, and it is the same place twice: **the loop the PRD says is the operator's whole surface is not closed.** S10 calls the divergence list *"The improvement loop, and nothing more"*, and FR82 requires a divergence item be *"actionable toward a specification change"* — but §7.2 excludes the in-product specification editor, and no requirement in §8.1 gives the Builder an edit path over an already-promoted specification (FR1–FR12 describe first-time authoring only; FR26 gives rollback, FR28 gives attribution, nothing gives *change*). The arc from observed divergence back to a promoted fix has a missing step in the middle. The same shape recurs for escalation: FR47 makes the Agent the sole owner of escalation and FR69 lets a business declare escalation a *success*, while §7.2 excludes the escalation inbox and O2 concedes the receiving side is *"entirely unspecified"* — so in V1 a correctly-escalated episode has nowhere to go, and no epic in §12 owns the destination.

### Findings

- **high** The improvement loop has no authoring step (§7.1 S10, §7.2, §8.1, FR82) — the divergence list is the operator's primary surface and must be *"actionable toward a specification change"*, but the in-product specification editor is out of scope and no FR covers re-opening or amending a promoted specification through the Builder. As written, V1 can detect that a specification is wrong and cannot state how it gets fixed. *Fix:* add a requirement to §8.1 that the Builder accepts an existing promoted specification plus a divergence item as input and produces a revised draft for promotion — or state explicitly in §7.2 that V1's edit path is a full re-run of the Builder, and accept the burden consequence against FR12.
- **high** Escalation is specified on the sending side only, and no epic owns the receiving side (FR47, FR69, FR74, §7.2, O2, §12) — escalation is a first-class and sometimes *successful* outcome, yet destination, notification and response expectation are unspecified, the escalation inbox is excluded from the operator surface, and E10's scope is denial classification plus the episode viewer. E11 (channel adapter) does not claim it either. O2 says *"Before the first business goes live"*, which is after V1 is declared complete per S11. *Fix:* either assign the minimum escalation delivery (one named destination per business, reached through the existing channel adapter) to E11 or E10 with its own FR, or move O2's revisit condition earlier and state that no business may be promoted until it resolves.
- **medium** §10.2's per-business criteria have no failure condition (§10.2 versus §10.3) — §10.3 states *"The thesis fails if"* for each measurement; §10.2 lists three directional shares with no falsifier, which makes the usefulness claim — the one §5 marks **Untested** — the weaker-instrumented of the two claims. The PRD's own §5 says a low share of requests reaching correct resolution would falsify it, but that sentence never becomes a §10.2 criterion. *Fix:* carry §5's usefulness falsifier into §10.2 as a directional failure condition (no number needed: "falls rather than rises across the sequence", "the validity-floor-adjusted share does not exceed the pre-build human-handled share").
- **medium** §7.4's reuse and migration posture is owned by no epic (§7.4, §12) — §7.4 is substantial and prescriptive (a reusable-as-is inventory, a concept-transfers-only list, *"Process-level isolation between deployed Agents is permanent"*), and E1–E12 contain no migration or port epic. The inherited-assets work is therefore unestimated and unassigned. *Fix:* add an E0/E13 for the port, or annotate the affected epics (E4, E5, E7) with the §7.4 assets they inherit.

## Done-ness clarity — thin

This is the weak dimension, and the weakness is concentrated exactly where the PRD says its risk is. The Core sections are strong: FR45 (*"exactly two exits… There is no third path and no silent degradation"*), FR53, FR55, FR57, FR63, FR65, FR70, FR71 and FR96 are each testable as written, several of them negatively testable, which is the harder and more useful kind. FR33's *"refused before it reaches the person"* and NFR3's *"rejected, not filtered — never a quietly empty result"* give an implementer an unambiguous pass/fail. FR96 is the single best-specified requirement in the document because it demands its own non-vacuity proof. An engineer could build §8.5–§8.8 from this PRD.

§8.1 is a different document. Five of its twelve requirements have no condition an implementer could evaluate: FR3's *"the number of questions asked is a function of what is missing, not of a template"* (how is this checked?), FR4's *"MUST actively interview"* (what counts as having interviewed — coverage of the seven named categories, or a judgement?), FR5's *"MUST surface contradictions"* (with what recall, against what corpus?), FR7's *"MUST probe explicitly for the gap between printed policy and practised policy"* (the single highest-value requirement in the document and the least falsifiable), and FR10's *"The Builder is itself agent-driven. The platform's Agent nature is visible on the Builder side"* which is a design statement, not a requirement — *visible* to whom, measured how.

The structural problem underneath them: **FR12 instruments the easy half.** It records wall-clock time and source-artifact count — Builder *cost* — and no requirement anywhere records Builder *yield*. §8.11's programme measures engine leaks, resolution strain, setup effort, usefulness and the validity floor; none of them asks whether elicitation surfaced the undocumented rules it was built to surface. A Builder that asks nothing and produces a thin specification scores *better* on FR12 and FR91 than one that interviews well. The cheapest fix available is a seeded-business fixture: a constructed business whose undocumented rules are known in advance, against which elicitation recall is measurable — which turns FR4, FR5 and FR7 from adjectives into a number without introducing any target the PRD has no baseline for.

Elsewhere, done-ness fails in a handful of specific, fixable places: FR14 is seventeen distinct expressive capabilities under one ID; the ship gate is named three ways with three different blocking semantics; and FR76 asserts a computability property that §10.3 contradicts.

### Findings

- **critical** The named highest-risk section has no done-conditions (§8.1: FR3, FR4, FR5, FR7, FR10) — the PRD states *"The hard problem is elicitation, not generation"* and E2 is *"Highest — this is the product risk"*, yet these five FRs are unfalsifiable as written while the settled Core is sharply testable. Story breakdown will produce stories for E2 that cannot be accepted or rejected on evidence. *Fix:* add one acceptance requirement to §8.1 defining elicitation yield against a seeded business fixture whose undocumented rules (prohibitions, thresholds, exceptions, routinely-granted exceptions, mandatory escalations) are known in advance — recall against that known set is the done-condition for FR4, FR5 and FR7, and needs no numeric target to be falsifiable ("the set is reported; items missed are named").
- **high** FR12 measures Builder burden and nothing measures Builder yield (FR12, FR91, §8.11) — both recorded quantities (wall-clock time, source-artifact count) reward a Builder that asks less. The product risk the PRD identifies is uninstrumented. *Fix:* pair FR12 with a yield measurement recorded per business (undocumented rules surfaced, contradictions surfaced, specification defects recorded at authoring time versus discovered later as runtime divergence — the last is the real signal and is already derivable from FR77/FR78).
- **high** FR14 is seventeen requirements under one ID (§8.2 FR14) — a single sentence enumerating identity, goals, forbidden goals, preconditions, reasoning aids, rules, authorities, capability grants, overrides, non-delegable decisions, askable/unaskable content, prohibited inferences, source precedence, evidence requirements, success definition and data lifecycle. None is individually traceable, acceptable or assignable, and E1's scope collapses to one line item. *Fix:* split into a table with one ID per expressive capability (FR14a–q or FR99+), keeping the "at minimum" framing and the deferral of the field schema intact.
- **high** The ship gate has three names and conflicting blocking semantics (FR20, FR22, FR25, FR95, §7.1 S8, §12 E3) — FR22 gates promotion on *"the specification's validation suite"*; FR95 makes that suite *"platform-owned, domain-agnostic"* and rules that *"Per-business authoring checks may warn; only platform-owned checks may block"*; FR25 makes a per-business *"self-test"* the *"unit of done for a built business"*; FR20 puts evaluation criteria *inside* the specification; S8 calls it *"Invariant evaluation checks plus per-business criteria"*. So per-business criteria are simultaneously the unit of done (FR25) and incapable of blocking (FR95). An implementer of the gate cannot resolve this, and FR12/FR91 both measure "time to a passing self-test" without knowing which artifact that is. *Fix:* name the two artifacts distinctly in §6 (platform validation suite = blocking promotion gate; per-business self-test = the business's own acceptance checks) and state explicitly whether a failing self-test blocks deployment, promotion, both or neither.
- **high** FR97 contradicts §13.2's concurrency paragraph (FR97, §13.2) — FR97 requires that *"Capability and extension code never blocks the request path, and every external client carries its own explicit timeout"*, while §13.2 states *"Whether the request path blocks on I/O is an implementation decision in E5/E8, not settled here."* These cannot both hold. §13.2's dependency-failure bullet also says per-adapter timeout policy *"is not specified in this document"* when FR97 specifies that each client must have one. *Fix:* update §13.2 to record that FR97 settles the blocking question and mandates per-client timeouts, leaving only the timeout *values* to E7.
- **high** FR76 overclaims computability (FR76, §10, §10.2, §10.3) — *"Every success criterion in §10 is computable from the episode record without a human reading a transcript"* is false for all three §10.3 measurements: engine leaks require an engineering judgement and a human classification (FR89), setup effort is wall-clock and artifact count recorded outside any episode (FR91), and resolution-model strain is an expressibility judgement (FR90). Separately, §10.2's *"Human corrections per hundred episodes"* has no source requirement anywhere in §8 — nothing requires a human correction to be recorded as an event. *Fix:* scope FR76 to §10.1 and §10.2 explicitly, and add a requirement that human corrections to an episode are recorded as typed events against the episode record (without which the §10.2 trend and §10.4's corrections-as-trend guard are both uncomputable).
- **medium** The operator surface is adjectival (FR79, FR82) — FR79 defines the primary surface as *"in substance, 'here is what your specification got wrong'"* and FR82 requires an item be *"actionable toward a specification change"*. Neither says what a divergence item contains. FR77 gives the classification and FR81 gives a closed vocabulary, which carries part of the weight, but the item payload — which episode, which denial, which specification element implicated, what the suggested change is — is unspecified for the one screen V1 ships. *Fix:* give FR82 a content requirement: each item names its episode, its denial class, and the specification element the Core consulted when it denied.
- **medium** FR84's channel is ambiguous (FR84, §7.1 S9, E11) — *"one channel adapter… inbound email / messaging"* names two materially different ingress mechanisms behind one slash. Email and chat messaging differ in identity, threading, delivery semantics and the retry/edit behaviours FR98 is specifically about. The §8.10 rationale paragraph argues from email's properties (asynchronous, the operator's real inbound queue). *Fix:* commit to email for V1 and state messaging as the first follow-on adapter (which FR85 already makes cheap), or state that both ship and accept two adapters in E11's scope.
- **medium** FR42's first guardrail is not reconciled with FR43 (FR42, FR43, FR33, NFR6) — the five zero-configuration guardrails open with *"do not invent business facts"*, while FR43 states *"no enforcement path may rely on the model respecting what it was told, and no guarantee in this document rests on prompt text"*, and NFR6 concedes semantic faithfulness is *"evaluated against a threshold, never guaranteed"*. The actual enforcement for grounded claims is FR33's provenance refusal, but §8.5 never links to it. An implementer reading FR42 alone will not know which guardrails are Core-enforced and which are evaluation-measured. *Fix:* annotate each of the five guardrails with its enforcement locus (FR33 for facts, authorization for authority and undeclared actions, FR71 for success claims, FR47 for escalation), which also demonstrates the FR43 discipline rather than asserting it.
- **medium** The request-kind taxonomy is in scope but unspecified and undeferred (FR40, §7.1 S1, §7.3, E4) — S1 puts *"Request taxonomy"* in V1 and FR40 requires the Agent emit *"one of a closed set of request kinds"*, but the set is neither enumerated nor listed among §7.3's deferrals, unlike the resolution-kind enumeration which is explicitly deferred to O4. Readers cannot tell whether this is settled in the Spine, deferred, or an omission. *Fix:* one line in §7.3 or a pointer to the Spine section that fixes it.
- **low** FR19's *"human-readable"* has no criterion while its reader is deferred (FR19, O1) — *diffable* is testable; *human-readable* is an adjective whose only possible test is a named reader, and O1 defers who that is. The FR19 consequence list (diffing, asserting, reviewing, reverting, attributing) is excellent and does most of the work. *Fix:* restate as "readable by the reviewer role O1 defines" so the dependency is explicit rather than latent.
- **low** FR35's staleness bound has no default and no missing-declaration rule (FR35) — freshness is declared per read and per contract, *"never globally"*, which is right; but nothing says what happens when a business declares nothing, or who validates that a side-effect-gating read was declared fresh. FR23 shows the pattern this should follow. *Fix:* add to FR24/FR95's check set that a read gating a side effect without a freshness declaration fails validation.

## Scope honesty — strong

Omissions are explicit and reasoned, and several are stated more sharply than a reader would require. §7.2 distinguishes *descoped* from *architecturally forbidden* (platform-initiated outbound is *"Architecturally forbidden, not merely descoped"*), and the tenancy line draws the exact seam that would otherwise be silently assumed: *"Tenant identity on every record is in scope (§9); tenant hosting and commerce are not."* §7.3's "deferred, not excluded" category is the right instrument for a PRD subordinate to a settled architecture, and its closing rule — *"the requirement states the guarantee and names no mechanism"* — is honoured consistently through §8 and §9 rather than invoked once.

§13 is the strongest scope-honesty artifact in the document. §13.1 concedes that neither claim is proven, that the four exemplars are *"constructions, not any real business's rules"*, and that no numeric threshold exists anywhere, by decision. §13.2 answers the operational half item by item with pointers to the requirement that covers each, and says *"unmeasured"*, *"not specified"*, *"deferred"* where that is the truth. NFR6's *"The product may not promise correctness of meaning"* and §3.4's statement that the public evidence is contaminated are both the kind of admission that costs something to write.

The one scope-honesty gap is a supply question rather than a disclosure one, and it sits on the falsification programme itself.

### Findings

- **high** FR92 requires real inbound requests from businesses the PRD concedes are constructions (FR92, FR88, FR94, §13.1) — the usefulness test *"runs against each business's real inbound requests"* for four exemplars that §13.1 states are *"constructions, not any real business's rules"*, while FR94 commits only to *"Two or three conversations with real small-business operators."* Two or three conversations do not supply four businesses' real inbound queues, and V1 completion depends on S11 which depends on FR92. The input to the only experiment that can falsify the usefulness claim has no identified source. *Fix:* state where FR92's requests come from — a real operator relationship for at least one of the four (with the other three running the architecture claim only), or a sourced corpus of real inbound messages — and mark the usefulness claim as tested on *n* businesses rather than four.
- **medium** No `[ASSUMPTION]` tags and no Assumptions Index (document-wide) — zero inline assumption tags and no index, while the document rests on several inferences the sources did not confirm: that the asynchronous channel removes the latency constraint rather than deferring it (§8.10 note, NFR13); that constructed exemplar specifications are a valid substitute for real business rules for the architecture claim (§13.1 concedes this for the usefulness claim only); that the commercial assumption *buyer = builder* holds (§2 states the product *"may assume this commercially"* with no tag); that the operator possesses the operational knowledge the Builder will elicit (§2's defining characteristic, which FR94 exists to test). Each is load-bearing and currently indistinguishable from established fact. *Fix:* tag these four inline and add a short Assumptions Index; §11's structure is already the right template.
- **low** The Builder's failure behaviour is unspecified (§8.1) — every §8 section states what happens on the unhappy path except §8.1. FR6 covers *unresolvable business behaviour* (record a defect, never improvise), which is the important case; nothing covers an unparseable source, a mid-session abandonment, or a source the operator uploads that contradicts an already-promoted version. *Fix:* one requirement for Builder-side degraded outcomes, consistent with FR46's distinguishable-by-the-caller rule.

## Downstream usability — adequate

This is a chain-top document feeding epics and stories, so traceability matters here. The mechanics are sound: FR1–FR98 are contiguous, unique and gap-free; NFR1–NFR18 likewise; O1–O9, S1–S11 and E1–E12 are all defined and referenced; §12 maps requirement groups onto epics and explicitly labels the ordering *"dependency, not a behavioural sequence"*, pre-empting the exact misreading the §6 vocabulary rules exist to prevent. §9's cross-references into §8 (NFR2↔FR55, NFR3↔tenant identity, NFR11↔FR57) resolve, and §13.2 cites the specific FR behind each operational answer, which makes the limitations section auditable rather than rhetorical. Sections pull out alone cleanly — §8.5 and §8.8 are each comprehensible without the surrounding document.

Two things will cost a downstream workflow real time. First, §6 claims *"this document uses no others for these concepts"*, but a substantial set of load-bearing nouns used in the FRs is absent from it — and a story-writing pass will have to invent definitions for them. Second, FR14's omnibus form (above) means E1's largest deliverable arrives at story breakdown as a single prose sentence.

### Findings

- **medium** §6 omits load-bearing nouns it claims to govern (§6, versus FR6/FR16/FR40/FR52/FR63/FR77–FR82/FR89/FR93) — not in the vocabulary table: *specification defect* (FR6, FR48, FR77, FR78), *divergence* and *divergence item* (§8.9, FR78, FR79, FR82), *decision authority class* (FR16, despite §7.1 S1 naming it), *request kind* (FR40), *consequence class* (FR52), *late result* (FR63), *engine leak* (FR89), *validity floor* (FR93), *self-test* and *validation suite* (FR22, FR25, FR95). Most are bold-on-first-use and defined inline, which partly compensates, but the two gate terms are exactly the pair that conflict (see Done-ness). *Fix:* extend §6 with these entries; it is the cheapest high-leverage edit in the document.
- **low** FR25 introduces *deployment* as a gate event alongside *promotion*, undefined (FR25 versus FR22, §6) — *"Each business passes a self-test before deployment"* while promotion is the §6-defined publishing event. Whether deployment is promotion, a step after it, or the channel adapter going live is unstated. *Fix:* use *promotion* if they are the same, or define *deployment* in §6.
- **low** E5 covers model tiering, which no requirement establishes (§12 E5, §7.3, addendum A1) — *"tiering expressible but unpinned"* appears as epic scope, while tiering exists in the PRD only as a §7.3 deferral and an addendum note that explicitly states it is *"not a V1 product requirement"*. An epic line item with no backing FR will generate a story with no acceptance criteria. *Fix:* either add a requirement that the runtime boundary must be able to express per-capability-class model selection, or drop the phrase from E5.
- **low** FR95–FR98 break ID/document ordering within their sections (§8.3, §8.6, §8.10) — §8.3's table now runs FR22–FR29 then FR95–FR96. Stable global IDs are the right choice and this is the correct trade, but a reader scanning for "the §8.3 requirements" will stop at FR29. *Fix:* note under §8's lead paragraph that IDs are append-only and not in document order, so later additions appear at the end of their section's table.

## Shape fit — strong

The shape is right. This is a platform capability spec subordinate to a settled architecture, written for the owner and the implementer, and it is formalized accordingly: requirements as guarantees and constraints, no UJs, operational rather than user-facing success criteria, and a vocabulary section doing the work a UX document would otherwise do. The absence of user journeys is correct and not an under-formalization — the primary user is a single operator role in a first-run authoring flow, the §2 role table carries the context a protagonist would, and §2's four-business table plus §10.3 carry the variation a UJ set would otherwise have to express. Equally, the PRD is not over-formalized: there are no invented personas, no NFR boilerplate, no metrics for their own sake.

Brownfield handling is good and improved mid-review: §7.4 now classifies the shipped voice system's assets into reusable / concept-only / not reusable / deliberately-not-carried-forward, and states the inherited constraint (*"Process-level isolation between deployed Agents is permanent"*) as a requirement-shaped fact rather than a preference. The distinction between what the existing code earned (*"an inventory of Core guarantees"*) and what it got wrong (*"a hand-rolled agent loop with the agency removed"*) is exactly the brownfield judgement the rubric asks for, and four of the lessons have become FR95–FR98 rather than staying as prose.

One shape consequence is worth surfacing even though it follows from a correct decision: with no UJs and a Requester known only by role, the person actually having the conversation has no requirements written from their side.

### Findings

- **medium** The Requester side of the product carries no requirements (§2, §8, O3) — the Requester is named as a real role, and every §8 requirement is written from the Agent's, Core's or operator's side. Nothing states what the Requester is told when an episode goes `pending` with a deadline (FR60), when a guard halts the episode (FR45), when escalation occurs (FR47), or whether they learn they are talking to an Agent (O3, open). On an asynchronous channel, an episode that silently goes pending for days is indistinguishable to the Requester from being ignored — which is the §3.1 problem the product exists to solve. *Fix:* add two or three Requester-facing requirements (acknowledgement, pending notification with its named external input, escalation hand-off notice) to §8.7 or §8.10; they are channel-neutral statements of obligation and do not require UJ apparatus.
